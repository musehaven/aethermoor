01

Round Flow

The game runs 8 rounds. Each round is four phases in this order. Most VP at the end wins; ties go to whoever has the most resources in storage.

  1. Action Phase. Starting with the Start Player and going clockwise, each player takes one Action or Pass per turn, looping until everyone has passed. The number of Actions you can take is limited only by how many Influence Discs you have.
  2. Combat Phase. Resolve every battle (sectors with opposing ships), then attack population, influence empty sectors, claim discovery tiles, and repair damage.
  3. Upkeep Phase. Pay your Civilization Upkeep Cost with Money income, then produce Materials and Science from your population.
  4. Cleanup Phase. Return Influence Discs from your Action Track, refill the Tech Tray, flip Colony Ships & Summary Tile face up, advance the Round Marker.
Passing The first player to Pass each round takes 2 Money and next round's Start Player Tile. After you've passed, on later turns you may only take a weaker Reaction (one Upgrade, Build, or Move activation — no tech bonuses apply) or Pass again.
02

The Six Actions

Every Action moves your leftmost Influence Disc onto that Action's space. You can repeat the same Action as many times as you have discs. The rightmost empty spot on your Influence Track = the Money you owe at upkeep, so more actions cost more upkeep.

EXPExplore

Flip the top Sector from the I, II, or III stack next to a sector you control or have an unpinned ship in. Then place it or discard it. A placed sector must connect a wormhole to where you explored from.

If it has a discovery symbol, add a face-down Discovery Tile. If it has Ancient symbols, stack that many Ancients on the tile. Undefended discovery tiles can be taken right away; you may then drop an Influence Disc to control the sector.

You can't take control of a sector with living Ancients — clear them in combat first.

RESResearch

Buy an available Tech Tile from the Tray by paying its Science cost. Place it on the leftmost open space of its track (Military, Grid, or Nano).

Each tech in a category gives a growing discount on the next one — but every tech has a minimum cost you always pay. Buy cheap-to-expensive within a category to maximize discounts.

Max 7 tiles per track. Can't cover printed starting techs, can't research a duplicate, can't discard a researched tile.

UPGUpgrade

Per activation: (1) return any number of ship parts to the tray, then (2) move one part from the tray onto any ship blueprint. Upgrading a blueprint upgrades every ship of that type instantly.

A blueprint's total Energy Consumption must not exceed its Energy Production. You need the matching researched tech for any non-basic part. Interceptors, Cruisers, and Dreadnoughts need at least one Drive; Starbases can't take Drives.

BUIBuild

Build one ship or structure per activation in a sector you control, paying its Materials cost.

Costs: Interceptor 3 · Cruiser 5 · Dreadnought 8 · Starbase 3 · Orbital 4 · Monolith 10. Starbases, Orbitals, and Monoliths need their tech first. One Monolith and one Orbital max per sector.

MOVMove

Move one ship up to its Movement Value (total Drive value) in sectors per activation, only across wormhole connections.

Pinning: each opponent ship in a sector pins one of your ships, and pinned ships can't leave a sector containing opponents. The GCDS pins everything in the Galactic Center.

INFInfluence

Per activation, move one Influence Disc — from your track or an existing sector — to take/drop control. Plus, this action flips up to two used Colony Ships face up again.

Removing a disc from a sector returns all its population cubes to your tracks. You can't use two activations in the same sector in one Influence action.

Draco activations Descendants of Draco get: EXP 1 · RES 1 · UPG 2 · BUI 2 · MOV 2 · INF 2 per action. (Your Explore flips two sectors — pick one or none.)
03

Colony Ships & Population

Controlling a sector doesn't produce anything on its own — you have to colonize the planets.

How Colonizing Works

Colony Ships are the only way to move population cubes onto planets. Any time during your Action or Reaction, flip a face-up Colony Ship face-down to move one cube from your track to a matching empty population square in a sector you control.

It's a deliberate move, not automatic — an uncolonized sector produces nothing even if you control it. You can also use remaining colony ships during Upkeep (but not into sectors with opponent ships).

Square Types

Gray squares take a cube from any track. Advanced squares (with a star) need the matching Advanced tech: Materials → Advanced Mining, Science → Advanced Labs, Money → Advanced Economy (or Metasynthesis for any).

Orbitals add a square that takes only a Science or Money cube.

04

Combat

Battles resolve in descending Sector Number order. In each sector, players fight two at a time in reverse order of entry — the last to arrive is the Attacker, and whoever controls the sector always defends last. Ancients, Guardians, and the GCDS always defend.

Die+ Computers− Shields≥ 6 = HIT

A burst symbol always hits; a blank always misses — no matter the bonuses. Everything else uses the formula above.

  1. Order & Initiative. Set attacker/defender, then activate ship types by Initiative (total chevrons on the blueprint, highest first). Ties go to the defender.
  2. Fire Missiles. All missiles fire once at the start, in initiative order. Roll one die per missile symbol.
  3. Engagement Rounds. Each ship type activates in initiative order and chooses to Fire Cannons or Retreat. Repeat rounds until only one side's ships remain.
  4. Damage. Each hit deals damage equal to its burst count. Damage over a ship's Hull value destroys it; lesser damage is marked with cubes and resets after the battle.
  5. Reputation Tiles. After all battles in a sector, each participant draws up to 5 tiles (1 for fighting, +1/+2/+3 per kill by ship size), keeps one, returns the rest.

Retreating

Declare a retreat on a ship type's activation by moving them to the edge toward a sector you control with no opponents. They complete the retreat on your next activation. Retreating ships can't attack but can still be attacked until they leave. If all your remaining ships retreat, you get no participation reputation tile.

vs. Ancients / GCDS Another player rolls for the non-player ships. Their hits are assigned to destroy your ships largest-first; if none can die, they deal max damage largest-first.
After battles Surviving ships attack enemy population cubes once with non-missile weapons (cubes count as 0 shields). Neutron Bombs tech wipes all cubes in a sector automatically.
05

Ships & Blueprint Parts

Every symbol on a blueprint is cumulative across all its parts. Bigger ships cost more but hold more parts.

Weapons

Each weapon symbol rolls one die of its color. Damage = the burst count shown.

Computers

Added to your die rolls to hit. Electron +1, Positron +2, Gluon +3.

Shields

Subtracted from enemy rolls to hit you. Gauss −1, Phase −2.

Hull

How much damage a ship absorbs before it's destroyed.

Drives

Total value = Movement Value. Drive chevrons also add Initiative.

Sources

Produce energy. Every part's consumption must stay under your total production.

One tech per part Each upgradeable part needs its own researched tech. Having Gluon Computer tech does not let you use Positron Computers — they're separate.

Species Cheat Sheet

No species has a special win condition — everyone races for VP. Each just has unique abilities and starting kit.

Our Table

Descendants of Draco

Start · 3 Mat / 4 Sci / 2 Money · Fusion Drive · Sector 228

+1 VP per Ancient left alive on the board at game end. Explore flips 2 sectors (pick 1 or none). You can share sectors with Ancients and even control them with a disc — but you can't fight Ancients or take discovery tiles from their sectors.

Eridani Empire

Start · 26 Money · Gauss/Fusion/Plasma · Sector 222

Draw 2 reputation tiles at setup. Start with 2 fewer influence discs. Ships have bonus energy production.

Hydran Progress

Start · 6 Science · Advanced Labs · Sector 224

Research 2 tech tiles per Research action. Start with a cube on an advanced science square.

Planta

Start · Starbase · Sector 226

+1 VP per controlled sector, explore 2 sectors per activation. But population is auto-killed by enemy ships and ships have lower initiative.

Mechanema

Start · Positron Computer · Sector 230

3 Upgrade activations per action, cheaper building costs across the board.

Orion Hegemony

Start · Cruiser · Neutron Bombs / Gauss · Sector 232

Start with a Cruiser instead of an Interceptor, higher initiative, bonus ship energy.

Frequently Asked

Pulled straight from the official rulebook FAQ. Tap to expand.

If I have Gluon Computer tech, can I use Positron Computer parts?

No. Each ship part needs its own tech (except the default parts: Ion Cannon, Nuclear Drive, Nuclear Source, Hull, Electron Computer).

If Draco clears Ancients but keeps control, do they get the discovery tile?

Yes — they take the discovery tile at the end of the Combat Phase.

I don't have enough Influence Discs. What can I do?

Use the Influence action to return up to two discs from sectors to your track. Research Advanced Robotics or Quantum Grid for more discs. Or deliberately overspend and return some at upkeep.

The good techs are so expensive. How do I afford them?

Techs in the same category discount each other. Buy them cheapest-to-most-expensive within a category to get the biggest total discount.

My ships keep dying. How do I win more battles?

Get an edge: upgrade Drives for initiative (shoot first), use Missiles before close combat, add Shields to blank out enemy computers, add Hull to survive, and grab Discovery ship parts.

Are Interceptors too small to bother with?

No — they shine when specialized (a better energy source lets them pack a punch), and they're great for pinning larger ships to waste opponents' actions.

Are Shields useful if my opponent has no Computers?

No. But if they have no computers, they probably can't hit you anyway (they'd need a natural burst). Use that to your advantage.

If all my ships retreat but die anyway, do I get a reputation tile?

No. Ships incur the retreat penalty the moment they attempt to retreat, whether or not they escape.

Can I move through an enemy sector with Cloaking Device without an Act of Aggression?

Yes — as long as your ships remain unpinned.

Do I have to use Draco's or Planta's double-Explore ability?

No. You can decide after seeing the first sector whether to flip a second one.

Can I remove population cubes without abandoning the sector?

Not directly. You can do it with the Influence action by removing the disc (which returns the cubes) and then re-placing the disc in a separate activation.

Where do I place things from an Ancient Labs discovery tile?

In your Starting Sector. If you don't control your Starting Sector, take the tile as 2 VP instead.

Common Mistakes at the Table

The rules people forget most often.

Disc Direction

Always spend your leftmost disc on an action. Returned discs go to the rightmost open space. The rightmost empty spot shows your Money upkeep.

Control ≠ Production

Controlling a sector alone produces nothing. You must colonize its planets with Colony Ships.

Energy Budget

A blueprint is illegal if consumption exceeds production. Check before you build or upgrade.

Missiles Fire Once

Missiles fire a single time at the start of the battle — not every engagement round.

Initiative Ties

Ties in initiative go to the defender, not the attacker.

Rep Tile Cap

You draw a maximum of 5 reputation tiles per battle, keep only 1, and return the rest.
